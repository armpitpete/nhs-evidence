## Major change: Covid-19 Inquiry Module 4 response

**Earlier position:** The Inquiry published its Module 4 vaccines/therapeutics report on 16 April 2026 with five recommendations; the government's formal response was outstanding.
**Later evidence:** The Department of Health and Social Care and Cabinet Office published a formal response on 16 October 2026, setting out its approach to all five recommendations.
**Observed transition:** inquiry recommendations awaiting formal response → government position published. **Current status: response published; implementation not verified.** Government says it will track commitments through the Inquiry implementation dashboard. This is an accountability milestone, not proof that recommended measures have been implemented.

### Recommendation-level comparison (source-level extraction, provisional)

1. **Standing pharmaceutical expert panel.** Inquiry sought an independent-chaired standing panel with substantial preparedness/coordination responsibilities. Government proposes a *modified* industry stakeholder advisory group with an advisory remit; strategic preparedness responsibility stays with government. **Response: modified proposal; establishment and effectiveness unverified.**
2. **Community networks and annual vaccine-equity reporting.** Government describes existing engagement, service-access work, a four-nations equity subgroup and plans for measurable action, but prefers alignment with existing reporting routes *rather than a single annual report*. **Response: partial/different reporting mechanism; comparable annual accountability unverified.**
3. **Vaccine uptake monitoring and evaluation.** Government describes existing surveillance, local-targeting and four-nations collaboration. **Response: ongoing activity and further alignment, but no newly evidenced common standard, completed evaluation or improved uptake attributable to this response.**
4. **Linked health records for safety surveillance.** Government commits to work with devolved governments and partners to improve access to linked data with confidentiality safeguards, while explicitly acknowledging the need for a framework, systems and investment. **Response: commitment; framework, funding, data access and privacy implementation unverified.**
5. **Vaccine Damage Payment Scheme reform.** Government accepts the need for reform and commits to reviewing options with affected people. It does **not** in this response establish the Inquiry's proposed inflation-adjusted amount, tiered awards or implementation timetable. **Response: reform review promised; detailed reform not delivered.**

**Explanation status:** The content of the response is established from the primary document; its eventual practical effects are unknown. No claim is made that any of the five recommendations has been fulfilled in substance.

**Investigation consequence:** Preserve each original Inquiry expectation alongside the narrower or different government response, then establish accountable bodies, promised milestones and observable implementation measures. Avoid turning a policy proposal into an outcome.

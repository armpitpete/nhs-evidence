# NHS Accountability Report — 2026-W40

**Evidence window: 28 September–4 October 2026.**

This report is a recovered weekly candidate because the routine daily receipts for the window were not durably captured. The recovery uses authoritative public evidence and does not fabricate daily execution history.

## Major changes

### Leeds maternity and neonatal review — review scope and timetable became concrete

**Earlier state:** an independent review had been announced and Donna Ockenden appointed.

**Later evidence:** DHSC published the terms of reference on 28 September. Clinical case reviews are due to begin in November; the review is expected to conclude by 2029 and covers severe harm/death cases from 1 January 2011 through 31 March 2028.

**Concrete transition:** announced review → defined scope/timetable.

**Current status:** **implementation-started / review established**.

**Explanation status:** established for the review machinery; no review findings yet exist.

**Yorkshire significance:** this is the strongest local accountability development in the week because it creates a defined mechanism against which later progress, family participation, findings and recommendations can be checked.

### Healthy Babies / Best Start family hubs — new national implementation commitment

**Earlier state:** Best Start family hubs were rolling out, but Healthy Babies services were not present in all local-authority areas.

**Later evidence:** on 30 September DHSC committed to expand Healthy Babies support to all local authorities and said enhanced support would be available through more than 1,000 hubs by 2028.

**Concrete transition:** partial/local coverage → announced universal local-authority rollout with an observable 2028 delivery horizon.

**Current status:** **announced**.

**Explanation status:** government-stated rationale is integration of infant feeding, mental-health, health-visiting, midwifery and family support; effectiveness is not established.

**Next test:** capture implementation milestones and eventual coverage rather than treating the announcement as delivery.

### CVD modern service framework — official impact estimates corrected

**Earlier state:** the July framework reported modelled potential impacts including over 60,000 events avoided, £1.3bn productivity gains and £1.2bn health/social-care savings.

**Later evidence:** the 2 October update corrected those figures to over 57,000 events avoided, £1.2bn productivity gains and £1.1bn health/social-care savings, and corrected two metric names.

**Concrete transition:** original published quantitative claims → formally corrected quantitative claims.

**Current status:** **corrected**.

**Why it matters:** longitudinal accountability requires retaining the correction as a state transition rather than silently replacing the original figures.

**Next test:** the framework retains a 25% ten-year ambition for premature heart-disease/stroke mortality and promises further implementation information later in 2026; those are the more consequential future delivery checks.

Absence of a full response is not classified as failure because the preserved evidence does not establish that its deadline has passed.
- **Martha's Rule:** remains **implementation-started**; Week 40 does not establish universal national completion across all settings.
- **ONS recurring weekly deaths publication:** the 30 September release occurred as scheduled; next release 7 October.
- **Healthy Babies rollout:** new announced implementation commitment with an observable 2028 horizon.
- **CVD framework:** published framework remains active but its modelled impact estimates were formally corrected on 2 October.

## Change Detection Review

Week 40 contains three material state changes: the Leeds review acquired a defined scope/timetable; Healthy Babies acquired a national rollout commitment; and the CVD framework's published quantitative impact estimates were corrected. The ONS recurring publication occurred normally. Thirlwall and Martha's Rule did not acquire a supported status transition.

## Failure Review

No new Week-40 item is classified as a demonstrated promise failure. The Leeds review exists because of serious maternity/neonatal safety concerns, but publication of its terms of reference does not itself establish the review's eventual findings. Missing Thirlwall completion evidence is not converted into non-delivery.

## Improvement Review

- The Leeds review moved from announcement to a defined investigation process.
- ONS delivered the recurring weekly deaths publication on 30 September.
- Healthy Babies gained a broader implementation commitment, but this remains prospective rather than proven improvement.

## Remedy Review

The Leeds review is an accountability/investigatory mechanism, not yet evidence that underlying maternity safety problems have been remedied. Healthy Babies is a policy intervention whose effectiveness remains to be tested. The CVD framework describes a delivery model and ambitions; its corrected modelled estimates are not outcome evidence.

## Evidence gaps

- Full government response to all 17 Thirlwall recommendations.
- Universal/setting-level Martha's Rule coverage and outcome evidence.
- Leeds review case-review progress and eventual findings.
- Healthy Babies implementation milestones between announcement and 2028 coverage.
- CVD implementation detail promised for later in 2026 and later outcome evidence against the 25% ten-year ambition.

## Yorkshire/local comparison

Leeds is the dominant Yorkshire development this week. The terms of reference provide a concrete future accountability path but no basis yet for judging whether care has improved. Existing York/Scarborough Martha's Rule inpatient evidence remains positive but does not establish ED or universal setting coverage.

# NHS Accountability Report — 2026-W39

**Evidence window: 21–27 September 2026.**

This report prioritises evidenced state transitions over publication volume. Missing public evidence is not treated as failure, and explanation is kept separate from observation.

## Major changes

### ONS September publication commitment — fulfilled

**Earlier state:** REC-NHS-2026-0013 preserved the confirmed September schedule: weekly provisional deaths on 23 September and Death Certification Reform material on 25 September.

**Later evidence:** REC-NHS-2026-0015 observed the 23 September weekly-deaths release. The 26 September bounded daily scan directly verified the scheduled 25 September Death Certification Reform release set; REC-NHS-2026-0016 records the resulting status transition.

**Concrete transition:** announced → partially-fulfilled → fulfilled.

**Current status:** **fulfilled** for the bounded publication commitment.

**Why it matters:** this is a clean positive accountability result: the observable publication promise was delivered on the recorded dates.

**Explanation status:** unknown / not required to establish delivery.

**What remains unknown:** publication completion does not establish whether Death Certification Reform has achieved its policy aims or whether the medical-examiner service is effective. ONS itself separates the statistical release from policy evaluation.

**Next check:** treat future weekly-deaths releases as new recurring events; assess DCR outcomes only against separate outcome evidence.

### Martha's Rule — implementation is real but national completion is not yet established

**Earlier state:** NHS trusts and foundation trusts must implement all three core components by 31 March 2027.

**Later evidence:** phase 3 has commenced; setting-level data collection began; aggregate programme activity shows active use; York/Scarborough public information establishes 24/7 adult and paediatric inpatient access. The captured national evidence still does not establish universal ED, maternity and neonatal availability.

**Concrete transition:** announced → implementation-started.

**Current status:** **implementation-started**.

**Why it matters:** there is positive delivery evidence without a basis for declaring the national commitment fulfilled.

**Explanation status:** established for the implementation activity; causal effectiveness remains unassessed.

**What remains unknown:** complete setting-level coverage, outcomes, and local ED availability/use.

**Next check:** WATCH-NHS-2026-0002 and the planned 1 October local/setting-level check.

### Thirlwall — immediate work is not the promised full response

**Earlier state:** on 15 September DHSC stated that government would consider the inquiry report in full and publish a full response after detailed consideration. NHS England separately required boards to review/follow up recommendations and immediate actions.

**Later evidence:** the bounded 26 September check still found the 15 September statement/immediate-actions material but no later full mapped government response to all 17 recommendations.

**Concrete transition:** no supported status transition for the full-response promise.

**Current status:** **announced**. The NHS organisational-action obligation is also not yet assessable as completed from the captured evidence.

**Why it matters:** immediate action must not be misreported as fulfilment of the separate full-response promise.

**Explanation status:** unknown.

**What remains unknown:** publication timing, underlying NHS England requirements, deadlines/assurance mechanisms, and implementation completion.

**Next check:** WATCH-NHS-2026-0001.

## Promise Review

The current promise ledger contains four preserved baselines. One has reached **fulfilled** (ONS September publication commitment); one is **implementation-started** (Martha's Rule); the government Thirlwall full-response promise remains **announced**; and the NHS organisational Thirlwall review/follow-up obligation remains announced with completion not yet assessable.

No commitment is classified as unmet solely because evidence is absent.

## Change Detection Review

The strongest verified Week-39 transition is ONS publication delivery. Martha's Rule retains its earlier implementation-started status; no evidence justifies promotion to fulfilled or demotion to delayed/unmet. Thirlwall retains announced status because the captured government source gives no full-response deadline and no full response has yet been observed.

There is not yet enough longitudinal evidence to claim a broad NHS direction-of-travel shift.

## Failure Review

### Mental-health crisis care in emergency departments

HSSIB evidence identifies prolonged waits, unsuitable environments, workforce pressures and wider pathway gaps. This is demonstrated national patient-safety problem evidence.

It is **not** currently a broken-promise finding in this repository because no matching accepted DHSC/NHS England delivery commitment with success criteria has been captured. The defined expectation required for a promise-failure judgment is therefore absent.

No additional Week-39 item is promoted to demonstrated failure merely from missing public evidence.

## Improvement Review

- ONS delivered the bounded September publication commitment.
- Martha's Rule has observable implementation activity and local York/Scarborough inpatient availability.
- Government has begun immediate Thirlwall-related work, but that positive activity remains distinct from completion of the promised full response.

## Remedy Review

HSSIB recommendations R/2026/097 and R/2026/098 remain **official-recommendation-level** remedy evidence for mental-health crisis care in emergency departments, covering operational/design standards, accountability, shared-care/escalation, workforce planning and measurement.

They are not presented as proven solutions and are not converted into government/NHS commitments unless an authoritative body accepts or operationalises them.

## Evidence gaps

- Full underlying NHS England Thirlwall requirements, deadlines and assurance mechanism.
- A full government response to all 17 Thirlwall recommendations.
- Universal/setting-level Martha's Rule coverage and outcome evidence.
- York/Scarborough ED Martha's Rule availability and comparable local mental-health crisis performance data.
- Outcome evidence sufficient to evaluate Death Certification Reform rather than merely its publication timetable.

## Yorkshire/local comparison

York/Scarborough evidence establishes 24/7 Martha's Rule access for adult and paediatric inpatients. It does not establish ED coverage, so no local failure is inferred.

For mental-health crisis care, the repository has local routing evidence but not enough comparable operational performance evidence to determine whether York/Scarborough reproduces, avoids or differs from HSSIB's national findings.

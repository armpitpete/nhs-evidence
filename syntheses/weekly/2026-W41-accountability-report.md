## Major changes

### Healthcare AI — accepted recommendations became trackable delivery promises

**Earlier state:** the National Commission had published recommendations for healthcare-AI regulation.

**Later evidence:** on 6 October the government accepted all 44 recommendations. The response says the MHRA intends draft guidance by December 2026 on managing changes to adaptive AI-enabled medical devices, and a full implementation roadmap with roles, responsibilities, timelines and key deliverables is to follow by Spring 2027.

**Concrete transition:** independent recommendations → government acceptance plus defined future implementation deliverables.

**Current status:** **announced / implementation machinery started**. AI Airlock Phase 3 and a Programme Board are evidence of activity, but neither fulfils the guidance or roadmap promises.

**Next test:** WATCH-NHS-2026-0005 checks the December guidance and later roadmap.

### Mental health, ADHD and autism — final review triggered a government reform package

**Earlier state:** the Fonagy review was underway and the system faced long waits and diagnosis-dependent access.

**Later evidence:** the final review and government response were published on 9 October. Government committed to a needs-led direction, stronger oversight of autism/ADHD assessment services, and an NHS England national-pricing consultation. NHS England intends finalised payment arrangements for NHS-funded autism and ADHD services from April 2027.

**Concrete transition:** independent review process → published final recommendations plus government response and a specific April 2027 payment-arrangement commitment.

**Current status:** **announced** for the payment reform. The wider needs-led direction remains policy intent until delivery evidence exists.

**Next test:** WATCH-NHS-2026-0006 checks consultation and final April 2027 arrangements.

### ONS weekly mortality publication — recurring release delivered

ONS published its weekly provisional deaths release on 7 October as scheduled. This is a successful recurring publication event. It does not reopen or duplicate the already-fulfilled September publication commitment.

# Linking NHS pressure datasets without assuming causation

## Signal

The captured bulletin lists multiple scheduled releases covering A&E activity, admitted and outpatient care, workforce, sickness absence, earnings, primary-care activity and other operational measures.

## Why this may be worth investigating

The releases may allow a joined-up view of service pressure over time rather than treating each statistic in isolation.

The purpose is exploratory: correlation between datasets must not be presented as causation.

## Questions

- Do A&E activity, workforce levels and sickness absence move together over comparable periods?
- Which measures show persistent regional or organisational divergence?
- Are changes in activity accompanied by changes in waiting, staffing or access measures?
- Which apparent patterns survive adjustment for seasonality and reporting changes?
- Are there Yorkshire-specific patterns worth deeper local reporting?

## Evidence that would strengthen the lead

- Repeated aligned trends across comparable official series.
- Organisation-level outliers reproduced across several releases.
- Primary documentation explaining a plausible operational link.

## Evidence that would weaken the lead

- Trends disappear when periods/definitions are aligned.
- Series use incompatible populations or definitions.
- Apparent changes are explained by methodology revisions.

## Limitations

This is a data-analysis opportunity only. No causal relationship is currently established.
